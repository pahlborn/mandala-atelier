Description

Make a mark. Draw a line. See what it becomes.

Mandala Atelier is not a colouring book. It is an instrument for symmetry.

You set down a dot, a line or a shape — and the atelier turns it into a pattern. Out of something simple, something manifold comes about in a few moments. You need no skill for it: the order is given to you.

How often is yours to decide. Six axes, eight, ten, twelve, sixteen or twenty-four — and, if you want it, a mirroring that doubles every movement once more. A single stroke can appear forty-eight times.

And you are allowed to discard. Twenty steps back, everything can be undone and done again — trying costs nothing here.

This is not about being finished quickly. There is no right and no wrong. You draw with your finger or with the Apple Pencil, slowly or on impulse, exactly or freely.

Or you begin with a blank sheet. Symmetry only, no template — then every form comes from you, and the atelier multiplies it. That is how motifs of your own come about, from the ground up.

If you would rather colour than draw, take one of the 26 templates. One tap sets colour into a field — and into all the other segments at the same time. The motifs lie in five worlds: geometric and classical, nature, Zen and mindfulness, the seasons, and a kids' corner.

Crooked circles are a matter of the tool, not of talent. The Shape tool therefore sets exactly the same five building blocks the templates are made of, and sets them precisely: ring, spoke, leaf, diamond, band.

The colours are muted, and they carry names: terracotta, ochre, petrol, moss, mist blue, sand, rust. Five colour worlds of fourteen pigments each — Earth Pigments, Northern Light, Dyer's Garden, Smoked Glass — and one you mix yourself.

No gamification. No stars, no trophies, no streaks. Whoever paints here wants to switch off, not to win.

Whatever is finished is given a title and stays in the gallery on your device. You can save it as a picture or print it on A4 — the lines alone, if you like, to colour in with real pencils.

No account. No advertising. No network. Nothing that comes about here leaves your device.

In the atelier

  • Blank sheet — symmetry only, no template: motifs of your own from the ground up
  • Number of axes freely chosen: 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 or 24
  • Mirroring can be switched on — up to 48 repetitions of one movement
  • Filling works on all axes at once, or on a single one
  • 26 templates in five worlds
  • Drawing with the finger or the Apple Pencil
  • Pen, fill, shapes (ring, spoke, leaf, diamond, band), eraser
  • Undo and redo, twenty steps deep
  • Five colour worlds of 14 muted pigments each, and one of your own
  • Light and dark
  • Zooming and panning with two fingers
  • Gallery on the device, every piece with a title
  • Print sheet in A4
  • Backup as a file — and with it the way onto a second device
  • Several people on one iPad, each with their own pieces
  • Full screen: the controls step back

Make your own room out of an empty surface.

How it goes

1 · Choose your mandala. One that speaks to you. There are simple ones and very dense ones. You don't have to know which is the right one. At the very top is the blank sheet — if you would rather begin with nothing.

2 · Begin at your own pace. Touch a surface or a line. Finger or Apple Pencil, both work. There is no set path through the mandala.

3 · Let the pattern come about. Draw a stroke and see what happens all around it. A small movement becomes a large form. You don't have to know beforehand what it should turn into.

4 · Play with colour. Choose one, try it, change it again. Combine — or stay with a few on purpose. There is no wrong colour.

5 · Take your time. A single surface carefully, or many areas on impulse. If your attention wanders off, simply come back to the next line.

6 · Lay the piece in the gallery. It is given a title and stays on the device. From there you can save it as a picture or print it.

7 · There is no finished mandala. Perhaps at some point it is exactly right. Perhaps it stays as it is. The way through the mandala matters more than its end.

Description

Take your time. Rub across the sheet. Let something come out.

The second atelier works the other way round from the first. There, a movement of the hand becomes a wreath. Here nothing is multiplied: the picture already lies in the sheet, and you bring it out.

You are given an empty sheet. You rub across it with your finger, and under your hand a drawing comes to light — the way one rubbed a coin through paper as a child. It doesn't come faster if you press. Only if you stay.

It is not about laying the sheet bare as quickly as possible. There is no right and no wrong, and no “done” that has to be reached.

The movement is the whole of the controls. No pen, no toolbar, no decision beforehand. Nine pigments lie ready under the sheet; it takes no more than that.

The sheet gives you a frame: a centre, recurring forms, and an order you can move freely inside. What lies within it you did not pick out — and that is exactly what takes the decision away from the beginning.

Rubbing has a sound, and the app makes it audible: filtered noise whose brightness and loudness hang on the speed of your hand — the sound of graphite on paper. There is no set rhythm. Whoever goes in a circle makes a period, and hears themselves doing it. Can be switched off.

The atelier is neither a competition nor a task.

You don't have to perform.
You don't have to reach anything.
You don't even have to rub the sheet to the end.

You can stop at any time and come back later. The sheet will still be lying there the way you left it.

In the atelier

  • Four kinds, for whatever you feel like today: Quiet, Bloom, Clarity, Abundance
  • Every sheet is made anew — no catalogue, no repetition
  • Four pigment worlds of nine colours each: Earth Pigments, Northern Light, Dyer's Garden, Smoked Glass
  • Rubbing with the finger or the Apple Pencil
  • Sound that hangs on the speed of the hand — can be switched off
  • Day and night: at night a tinted paper with pale chalk lying on it
  • Laying a sheet aside and taking it up again — the stack stays
  • A quiet surface that steps back: the controls sit behind a small mark in the corner
  • No account, no advertising, no network

One tip. On bare glass the finger only glides. Whoever has a matte film on their iPad — one that feels like paper — will feel the resistance while rubbing, and the app becomes a different one. It is worth it.

Make your own room out of an empty sheet.

How it goes

1 · Take a sheet. You are only asked what you feel like today — Quiet, Bloom, Clarity or Abundance. Which picture lies underneath, you find out by rubbing. You don't have to know which is the right one for you.

2 · Begin at your own pace. Lay your finger down and rub. Finger or Apple Pencil, both work. There is no set path across the sheet.

3 · Let the picture come out. It doesn't come faster if you press — only if you stay. Out of many single strokes, a drawing that hangs together gradually forms. You don't have to think about what it should turn into.

4 · Change the pigment. Nine lie ready. Take one, try it, take another. Or stay with a single one on purpose. There is no wrong colour.

5 · Take your time. You can work one place out carefully, or go across the whole sheet in wide sweeps. If you notice your attention wandering off, listen to the sound — it follows your hand.

6 · Break off whenever you like. The sheet stays lying there, even across a restart. If you want, lay it aside and take it back out of the stack later. Or begin a new one.

7 · There is no finished sheet. A sheet can keep changing as long as you work on it. Perhaps at some point it is exactly right. Perhaps you will want to rub on tomorrow. Perhaps it stays as it is. The way across the sheet matters more than its end.

Was daran diskutabel ist

  • Leaf ist die schwächste Stelle der Übersetzung Das deutsche „Blatt" ist beides zugleich: das Papier und das Pflanzenblatt, und die App lebt von genau dieser Doppeldeutigkeit. Leaf hat sie im Englischen auch – aber schwächer: Für Papier ist es dort altertümlich (a leaf of a book). Page träfe das Papier genau und verlöre die Pflanze ganz. Beide Namen sind im US-Store frei. Das ist eine Entscheidung, keine Übersetzungsfrage.
  • „Du musst nichts leisten" Steht als You don't have to perform. Wörtlicher wäre achieve, aber das steht schon in der nächsten Zeile (reach anything). Perform trifft daneben den Beiklang von Leistung, um den es geht.
  • Die Namen der vier Blätter Ruhe, Blüte, Klarheit, Fülle stehen als Quiet, Bloom, Clarity, Abundance. Abundance ist das unsicherste – es klingt im Englischen leicht nach Ratgeberliteratur. Fullness wäre nüchtern, aber im Zusammenhang mit Bildern missverständlich.
  • Diese vier Namen stehen auch in der App Und dort sind sie deutsch. Wer den englischen Store-Text liest und dann „Ruhe, Blüte, Klarheit, Fülle" vorfindet, findet nichts wieder.

Description

A child, an iPad, a day at the beach — and suddenly an hour has gone by.

In the studio your child learns to draw. Not from instructions in a book, but stroke by stroke: the template stands on the left, and the stroke whose turn it is glows red. On the right the child draws it. Five or six steps later, out of nothing, a dolphin has come about — and the child drew it themselves.

That is the moment this is all about.

29 motifs, from easy to tricky. Heart, shell and ice cream to begin with. Dolphin, lighthouse and seahorse once it is going better. Pirate ship, octopus and rocket for those who can already do it. Whoever would rather colour in than copy switches over and gets the same motifs as finished outlines.

And then there is the treasure hunt.

Here it turns serious — in the nicest sense. You hide something real: a shell, a voucher for an ice cream, a small surprise. In the parents' area you enter where it is, along with a photo and a clue.

Once your child has drawn twenty different motifs, the treasure chest opens: with your photo, your clue and a button — “I'll show you the way.” Then a child runs across the beach looking for a treasure they have earned. Nobody forgets that in a hurry.

Whoever doesn't want to wait unlocks the map in the parents' area straight away.

What children love as well

  • Medals for five, ten and twenty motifs — bronze, silver, gold
  • Eight levels, from Beach Child to Ocean Child
  • A collector's album with stickers
  • A friendly voice that reads out every step
  • Quiet music and the sound of the sea in the background
  • Inventing a motif of your own and setting the steps yourself
  • A gallery where every finished picture stays

And what puts parents at ease

  • Runs without a network — on the plane, on the beach, in the car
  • No advertising. No purchases inside the app. No chat. No account.
  • Nothing leaves the device — not even the child's first name
  • Several children, each with their own profile and their own gallery
  • German and English, switchable

Where the motifs come from. From Albarella, an island in the Po delta: seahorse, crab, flamingo, white poplar. Along with a small part to learn from — why the island is called that, where its sand comes from. Whoever holidays there will find it again. Whoever doesn't gets to know an island.

For children from about five years old.

How it goes

1 · Who is drawing today? Tap your name. If you don't have one yet, tap New child, write your name and pick an animal. Every child has their own studio — your pictures stay yours.

2 · Find yourself a motif. The easy ones are at the very top: heart, shell, ice cream. Further down it gets trickier — dolphin, lighthouse, pirate ship. Start wherever you like.

3 · Draw the red stroke. On the left you see the template. One stroke in it glows red: that one's turn it is. Draw it on your sheet on the right. That is all there is to it.

4 · Then the next one. Tap Next, and the next stroke lights up. After five or six steps your picture is finished. Something slipped? Back takes the last stroke away, the eraser does the rest.

5 · Take your time with the colours. Thirty-six lie ready, along with four pen widths. With the bucket you fill a whole area, with magic it turns colourful. There is no wrong colour.

6 · Lay your picture in the gallery. It stays lying there, and you can look at it again whenever you like. Whoever would rather colour in than copy switches to Colour in at the top — the same motifs, already as outlines.

7 · And then comes the collecting. For five motifs there is bronze, for ten silver, for twenty gold. Along with stickers for your album, and you move up — from Beach Child to Ocean Child.

For parents: the treasure hunt. Once the child has drawn twenty different motifs, the treasure island opens. Before that, enter a place, a photo and a clue under the gear wheel at the top — then the treasure chest shows the child the way to something you have really hidden. Whoever doesn't want to wait presses and holds the treasure-island button; then the map is open straight away.

Was danach noch fehlt

  • Die Texte durchsehen und freigeben Nichts hiervon ist beschlossen. Erst wenn Sie sie freigeben, gehören sie in App Store Connect.
  • Zwei Namensentscheidungen Leaf oder Page bei der zweiten App, und ob der dritten der Hinweis aufs Malen im Namen fehlen darf.
  • Die Apps selbst schalten um Gebaut: sprache.js je App, der deutsche Text ist darin der Schlüssel. Im Mandala Atelier sind es 34 Motive mit Beischrift, sechs Welten, 31 Bereichsnamen der Anlagen und 50 Pigmente; im ruhigen Blatt dazu die fünfundzwanzig Sätze, die dort das Einzige sind, was gesprochen wird. tools/test-sprache.js sucht nach Deutschem, das stehen geblieben ist – zuletzt: nichts.
  • Eine Zahl in der App stimmt nicht Unter den Motiven steht „19 Vorlagen in 5 Welten“, in der Beschreibung stehen „26 Vorlagen in fünf Welten“. Gezählt am laufenden Programm sind es 34 in 6. Das ist Ihr Text, deshalb habe ich ihn wortgetreu übersetzt und nichts stillschweigend berichtigt – aber vor dem Einreichen muss die Zahl stimmen: Richtlinie 2.3.1 verlangt zutreffende Angaben.
  • Die Datenschutzerklärung Liegt nur auf Deutsch vor. Sie wird je Sprache verlinkt und sollte englisch danebenstehen.
  • Der Name unter dem Symbol Den schaltet die App nicht um – er kommt aus dem Bundle. In Xcode braucht es dafür eine InfoPlist.strings mit CFBundleDisplayName, deutsch und englisch. Die Schritte stehen in der LIESMICH.md, die tools/gen-nativ.js mitschreibt.
  • Britisch oder amerikanisch? Alles hier ist britisch geschrieben – colour, centre. Das passt zu atelier und zum ruhigen Ton, aber der größte Store ist der amerikanische. Umstellen wäre ein Dutzend Wörter, an einer Stelle je App. Eine Entscheidung, kein Aufwand.
